# Limits and characteristic failures

[Contents](README.md) · [Previous: judging an abstraction](06-evaluating-abstractions.md) · [Next: preservation and transfer](08-preservation-and-transfer.md)

## Universal reference does not mean universal theorem

The ambition of this collection is wide usefulness. It does not assert one theory of every subject, a context-free essence behind all words, or a method guaranteed to generate discovery.

Logical, empirical, causal, procedural, and normative claims have different conditions of adequacy. A correct mathematical deduction cannot establish an empirical premise or authorize an action. A decision to pursue an approach does not make its assumptions true.

## Three different obstacles to understanding

A question can remain unresolved for different reasons:

1. Available evidence does not determine the answer.
2. A representation discards a distinction that the evidence contained.
3. The answer is determined, but discovering or computing it remains difficult.

More computation cannot recover information that was discarded everywhere without new input. A better encoding cannot decide an empirical question left open by the evidence. More observations may be unnecessary if the actual difficulty is a construction already determined by the premises. Diagnose the obstacle before prescribing generality.

## Agreement of observations is not agreement of interventions

Let $H$ be a fair hidden bit. Consider two supplied causal models:

- Model A: $X=H$ and $Y=X$.
- Model B: $X=H$ and $Y=H$.

Both produce $(X,Y)=(0,0)$ or $(1,1)$ with equal probability. Their observational distributions are identical. Under an intervention that replaces the equation for $X$ by $X=0$, Model A has $Y=0$, while Model B retains $Y=H$. The expected values of $Y$ are respectively $0$ and $1/2$.

A method given only the observational distribution receives identical input in the two cases. It cannot be guaranteed to return the two different correct intervention answers without further assumptions or evidence. A prior can prefer one model, but that preference is additional information.

This is a constructed example of underdetermination, not a claim that causal identification is always impossible. It prevents predictive fit, interpretation, and intervention validity from collapsing into one notion.

## Similarity is not equivalence

At tolerance $0.1$, the exact values $0$, $0.09$, and $0.18$ have adjacent pairs within tolerance while the endpoints are not. Threshold similarity is not transitive.

Taking connected components of a similarity graph therefore creates a different relation from the original threshold. That can be a useful deliberate clustering rule. It cannot silently become semantic identity, interchangeability, or a canonicalization guarantee.

## Analogy requires a declared strength

A comparison can guide a question without warranting transfer. A diagram shared by two domains may omit decisive differences in mechanisms, evidence, or allowed operations. A causal claim requires more than a common shape; a theorem application requires its hypotheses.

The mathematical vocabulary in this collection is not an instruction to label organizations as sheaves, observations as a complete Yoneda profile, or changes of circumstance as proven base change. An analogy remains useful when its role and boundary are clear.

## Formal correctness is not adequacy of interpretation

A proof checker can validate a formal consequence without deciding that the formal statement expresses the intended question. An exact computation can confirm consequences of invented inputs without establishing that the inputs describe reality.

Likewise, a formulation of desired behavior does not supply its existence, construction, efficiency, or applicability. Naming the operation "discover the useful abstraction" only restates an objective unless a method and evidence are supplied.

## Richness is not maximal retention

The nilpotent and symmetry examples explain why particular coarse views lose useful structure. They do not justify collecting every detail or building a universal ontology before encountering a concrete problem.

Retain a distinction because it can change a named consequence, support a required operation, or make correction possible. A richer representation is warranted when it improves the total account. A smaller one is warranted when its losses are harmless for the declared uses.

## Patience is not indefinite preparation

A sustained investigation may require substantial work before the main result. Its developing account should still show clarified dependencies, informative failures, constructions, or better questions.

If the account and next discriminating contact never change, calling the delay incubation adds no evidence. Direct attack, a conventional solution, or abandoning the line can be appropriate. No loyalty is owed to the rising-sea metaphor.

## Sources have different authority

Autobiography is testimony, often retrospective and philosophical. A colleague's account adds technical and historical context but is not an independent causal experiment on discovery. Several accounts quoting one passage do not constitute several independent observations.

Historical judgments about an unfinished programme belong to their dates. They should not be silently converted into claims about current theorem status. The sources also describe antecedents and collaboration; they do not make Grothendieck the sole inventor of category theory, sheaves, or local-to-global reasoning.

## Consequential use adds obligations

Exploration can entertain uncertain assertions. Reliance by others requires evidence appropriate to the consequences. A proposal is not acceptance, and acceptance of a conclusion is not necessarily permission to act.

Abstraction does not guarantee anonymity, eliminate confidentiality, or transfer source rights. A generalized artifact may retain identifying or restricted information. Private data, empirical interventions, publication, and commitments require their own authority; methodological interest does not supply it.

## What a useful outcome can be

A sound construction, a narrower theorem, a counterexample, a better question, a rejected interpretation, and a justified decision to stop can all improve understanding. Record which result occurred.

Elegance should emerge from an account that survives contact with its subject. When a beautiful picture and the evidence disagree, revise the picture.
