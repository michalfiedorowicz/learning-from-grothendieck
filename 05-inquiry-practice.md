# Inquiry as a working practice

[Contents](README.md) · [Previous: mathematical examples](04-mathematical-examples.md) · [Next: judging an abstraction](06-evaluating-abstractions.md)

The practices in this chapter are a proposed adaptation of the preceding lessons. They are not commandments extracted from autobiography or a mandatory sequence for every investigation.

## Two coupled forms of inquiry

In fixed-question inquiry, the intended task and criterion of adequacy remain sufficiently stable to compare methods. One can ask whether a new representation supports a more accurate answer, a simpler construction, or lower total cost for that task.

In question-forming inquiry, the subject presses for a revision of the task, relevant object, or adequate answer. The comparison should identify a concrete obstruction: what the old account could not express, what the replacement preserves or loses, and which question or construction has become possible.

The two forms inform each other. An example can expose a bad question; a new question can suggest a discriminating experiment; the experiment can reveal a missing distinction. There is no obligation to finish an ontology or polished theory before encountering examples. There is also no obligation for every intermediate concept to produce an immediate speedup.

A better question is not a solved problem. If the criterion changes, say so. Conversely, a benchmark for the old formulation cannot measure every gain in conceptual understanding. Keep both the earlier obligation and the reason for the revision visible.

## Choose a move by the uncertainty it can resolve

Begin with a live question and the best current account of it. Identify the uncertainty whose resolution would most change the inquiry. That may be a missing definition, an unsupported empirical premise, a construction that does not compose, or an apparent contradiction between sources.

Then choose the appropriate contact. A proof can settle a conditional consequence. A counterexample can defeat a universal claim. Reading can establish what an author actually argued. An authorized observation can bear on whether a model describes a real situation. No one kind of contact silently substitutes for the others.

A practical cycle is to articulate the question, compare serious rival accounts, name the conceptual move, develop its consequences, expose dependencies, and revise against evidence. Its order can change. The point is to keep the account corrigible, not to populate a form.

## Develop a concept through difficult instances

Carry a normal instance through the proposed account, then an unlike instance and a case that strains it. These are roles for examples, not an obligation to manufacture exactly three tests.

Ask which distinction each instance exercises. A case is useful when it could reveal a loss of information, a missing assumption, incompatible components, or a newly expressible question. Several cosmetically different examples may exercise the same path and add little.

A contrary case should be allowed to change the conclusion. A similar-looking situation with a broken precondition is often more informative than another easy success. A justified rejection, conditional answer, or request for missing evidence can be a better result than an unsupported affirmative answer.

## A complete hypothetical transformation

### Start with an object that hides the actual obligation

Suppose the starting idea is a tool that writes better descriptions for a public catalog. That names a mechanism and output, but not necessarily the result that matters. Excellent text can coexist with an inaccurate catalog because source records are stale, variants are mismatched, stock has changed, or nobody is authorized to publish the update.

Reframe the question: under what conditions can someone rely on a specified catalog being correct when it matters? The new formulation does not yet establish a viable service or technical solution. It exposes work that text generation alone did not cover.

### Separate roles and context

Distinguish the source of truth, transformation, reviewer, publication authority, affected catalog, acceptance criterion, and continuing responsibility. One person may occupy several roles; several people may divide them. The common role structure does not make those arrangements identical.

Also state the context: frequency of change, quality of inputs, update deadline, allowed error, existing synchronization, and availability of an approver. Changing one condition can reverse feasibility or usefulness.

A representation that records only approved text merges two consequentially different situations: one includes permission and an undertaking to publish, while the other does not. Ability, approval, authority, and completed action are different facts.

### Enlarge the comparison without presuming the answer

Compare a writing assistant, an existing synchronization mechanism, a staff procedure, a managed update process, and leaving the current arrangement unchanged. Compare them by the required condition and total remaining work, rather than by feature count.

The reformulation may reveal a stronger intervention or show that the existing substitute already suffices. Either is a useful outcome if supported by the situation. It should not protect the initial tool by redefining every alternative as inadequate.

### Recombine partial investigations

| Partial question | Dependency that must survive recombination |
| --- | --- |
| Which inaccuracies matter, to whom, and when? | The delivery method must meet that same acceptance standard |
| Which inputs are reliable, and who may authorize changes? | Automation cannot assume cleaner data or broader permission |
| What happens to ambiguous variants or conflicting values? | Exception work belongs in deadlines, staffing, and cost |
| What changes after an initial correction? | Continuing responsibility must correspond to continuing need |
| What does the current substitute already accomplish? | The claimed improvement must survive the actual comparison |

Suppose a proposed process meets the deadline only when there is no ambiguity, while the motivating need arises precisely from frequent ambiguity. The local delivery result and the description of the need do not fit together. That is a failure of the proposed construction, not merely a presentation problem.

### Seek a discriminating observation

A recent authorized input-to-publication episode could disclose actual exceptions, approval delays, and residual work. It would address a different uncertainty from asking whether someone likes the idea of an intelligent writing tool.

A contrary case is a catalog whose existing integration updates reliably with negligible effort. A one-time cleanup with no subsequent change defeats an assumption of recurring work. These are hypothetical contrasts, not collected evidence or instructions to contact anyone.

Possible conclusions include a narrower result, a different process, rejection of the initial idea, or a better question. The representation is useful if it makes these outcomes distinguishable and supports an appropriate next contact.

## Separate evidence, assumptions, and commitments

| Standing | Meaning | A common misuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed | What a named source or exercised situation actually disclosed | Treating advertised performance as independently measured benefit |
| Assumed | A premise or input supplied for a model | Treating a calculation as confirmation of its inputs |
| Derived | A consequence conditional on stated premises | Promoting internal validity into empirical adequacy |
| Conjectured | A claim awaiting adequate contact | Reporting an attractive explanation as a finding |
| Decided | An authorized choice | Treating the choice as evidence that its consequences are favorable |
| Unknown | A material answer that is missing | Filling the gap with a confident default |

These distinctions should clarify the account, not turn every sentence into administrative markup. The consequential claim should make its basis, scope, strongest relevant contrary case, and reopening condition recoverable.

## Keep a compact record that another reader can use

A useful research entry contains the concrete question, the evidence or source, the proposed conceptual move, a consequence or construction, a contrary case, remaining uncertainty, and the next useful contact. Include only what explains or supports the work.

Separate the current exposition from the few revisions that explain it. A failed assumption may be worth retaining; every abandoned sentence is not. If another reader cannot reconstruct why the distinction matters, the record is too thin. If the point is buried in a chronological diary, it has not been distilled.

When handing work to someone else, convey the substance of the puzzle and why it matters, not merely the desired answer. A collaborator should be able to challenge the framing rather than only execute it.

## Continue, simplify, or stop

Continue a line of abstraction when it removes an ambiguity, exposes a contradiction, connects evidence under justified assumptions, enables a construction, or produces a more discriminating question. Assess a sustained thread across its actual problem family rather than demanding an immediate payoff from every intermediate step.

Reconsider elaboration when it changes neither the account of the subject nor the contact that could advance it. A framework can be simplified, split, enlarged, or abandoned. Sunk effort is not a preservation criterion.

Stopping is relative to what must be answered. An adequate decision need not identify every possible model. Conversely, two observations that are individually unhelpful can become decisive together, so a rule to stop after every locally unproductive step also needs justification. Keep the purpose, unresolved dependency, and reason for continuing explicit.
